## In the Spotlight 🔦

Deno team is officially [joining Cloudflare](https://deno.com/blog/cloudflare) and this might be the end of road for Node.js killer. It's not void, I thought we can talk about the legacy of Deno and what it enabled.

- Deno was created by Ryan Dahl, the same developer who built Node, and its name contains the same letters as Node.
- The inspiration from comes from this talk that Ryan Dahl gave in 2018: [10 Things I Regret About Node.js - Ryan Dahl - JSConf EU](https://youtube.com/watch?v=M3BM9TB-8yA)
- Overtime, many of the features have made their way into Node.js, first implemented by Deno

  - built-in fetch and web APIs, a test runner, a permission model
  - TypeScript type stripping on by default, single-executable apps, `--watch` and `--env-file`
  - Deno meanwhile spent 2022–2024 adding npm and Node compatibility. In Dahl’s words, it was “sucked into the gravity well of node compatibility.”
  - **Ecosystem and products:** Beyond Node, Deno’s main legacies are:
  - web-standard server APIs, now formalized as Ecma TC55/WinterTC, which a Deno engineer chairs
  - the “one binary does everything” toolchain that Bun copied and extended
  - JSR, and Rust/V8 infrastructure (rusty_v8, deno_core, dprint-based formatting)
  - **Dahl himself gave up on the runtime thesis.** On Hacker News he called the decision “a joint decision and I agree with it.” His reasoning: “There are some good ideas in Deno and it’s well engineered - but it ultimately is not solving big problems. It has been sucked into the gravity well of node compatibility, which forces it to behave exactly as Node does. Why reimplement Node? It works. Marginal performance or UX or security benefits are not enough.”
  - Deno team tried many products before they arrived at the one that got them hired at Cloudflare: [celld](https://celld.dev/) - self hostable durable objects.

## In Other News

### [How AI agents shape experts’ judgment calls - Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-judgment-calls)

Anthropic conducted an experiment in which it gathered several experts in a room and provided them with an AI to shape their opinions on a topic. The AI received an initial bias on the topic, determined by a coin toss performed by Anthropic. When the experts were asked for their opinions, most aligned with the AI's stance, some repeating its reasoning verbatim.

Anthropic decided the AI's opinion through a coin toss for this experiment, but how much of it is decided beforehand through the training data?
