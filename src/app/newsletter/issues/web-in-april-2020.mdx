April is ending and the world is still in quarantine.

[Everything I said about remote working during a pandemic](https://buttondown.email/agney/archive/web-in-march-the-mindless-newsletter-by-agney/) still stand, but remote work is certainly gaining momentum in at least some of the companies. TCS, an Indian service company employing 0.5 million people have announced that [75% of their workforce will be going remote by 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/current/corporate/post-coronavirus-75-percent-of-3-5-lakh-tcs-employees-permanently-work-from-home-up-from-20-percent/story/401981.html). FYI has published a report analysing [The Future of Remote Work](https://usefyi.com/future-of-remote-work) estimating by 2025, 70% of the workforce will work remotely at least five days in a month. In other news, Uber CTO is leaving the company and they might lay off [20% of their workforce](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/28/thuan-pham-who-fled-vietnam-as-a-child-and-became-ubers-cto-in-2013-is-leaving-the-company/).

The release for [is-remote](https://www.npmjs.com/package/is-promise) got people in JavaScript community talking. This library is a one liner that determines if a variable is a promise or not. Everything was fine until one day the maintainer made a minor release for supporting ES Modules for new NodeJS update and [broke a whole lot of packages](https://github.com/then/is-promise/issues/13). Apps that broke include Serverless and create-react-app. People have been talking about the heavy reliance of third party libraries and how the number of dependencies can kill your app when maintainers make mistakes. There is a [post mortem for incident on Medium](https://medium.com/javascript-in-plain-english/is-promise-post-mortem-cab807f18dcc) which is an interesting read.

## Releases

- [Last Python2 version](https://stackoverflow.blog/2020/04/23/the-final-python-2-release-marks-the-end-of-an-era/?cb=1) - End of an era.
- [Github is free for Teams](https://github.com/pricing) - A Microsoft enterprise is waiving off all fees for teams. While this is indeed great for indie programmers there are growing concerns that Github might lose their way with only enterprise customers paying for the product.
- [Gatsby - Incremental Builds](https://www.gatsbyjs.org/blog/2020-04-22-announcing-incremental-builds/) - As the number of pages increases, it used to becomes painful to build a static app, every page has to get rebuild even if there is only small change. With Netlify's build minutes, this was a question on everyone's minds. GatsbyJS is now introducing a beta into incremental builds on their platform offering 1000x build speeds.
- [GraphQL Playground](https://foundation.graphql.org/news/2020/04/03/web-based-graphql-ides-for-the-win-how-why-playground-graphiql-are-joining-forces/) - [Prisma](https://www.prisma.io/) donated Playground to GraphQL foundation and now they are combining GraphiQL and Playground.
